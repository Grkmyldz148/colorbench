ColorBench Leaderboard

Color spaces ranked on real psychophysical datasets - from colour-science and helmlab. Lower score = closer to human vision.

Two boards, and each carries only its own metrics. Measurement = difference / discrimination (how accurately the space represents human color difference) - metricspace's home. Generation = hue-constancy + even spacing + robustness (what actually decides whether gradients and palettes look right) - genspace's home. A space that's great at measuring difference (e.g. DIN99) can be poor at generation, and vice-versa, so we never mix the two.

Measurement ranks every entrant on its human-color- difference accuracy, each with its OWN ΔE, one equal vote per dataset: it spans two regimes. Suprathreshold = the magnitude of clearly-perceptible differences (ΔE ~1-10): difference prediction = STRESS (hover for CI95) on 5 sources (COMBVD's BFD-P / Leeds / Witt / RIT-DuPont + held-out MacAdam 1974). Threshold = just-noticeable JND-ellipse roundness: discrimination / 3-D / tolerance. A model can lead one regime and not the other - metricspace, fit to suprathreshold COMBVD, tops Difference; the CAM "uniform color spaces" are built for threshold isotropy and lead discrimination. A pure distance model (metricspace = learned distance, CIEDE2000 / CIE94 = formulas) is scored on the same ellipses with its own distance, so it competes on those columns too - not blank. Overfit Δrank (metricspace only) = held-out rank - mean in-sample rank; on the held-out MacAdam it does not win. Generation ranks on the properties that actually decide synthesis quality: hue-constancy (Hung-Berns, Ebner-Fairchild, Munsell, Xiao - gradients keep their hue) and even spacing (OSA-UCS - no banding), one vote per dataset. Difference / discrimination metrics are NOT here - they judge color-matching, not generation (a DIN99, great at difference, makes ugly hue-shifting gradients). Greyed columns are shown, not scored: appearance diagnostics (Helmholtz-Kohlrausch brightness ρ ↑better / object lightness, chromatic adaptation - where CIELAB with no CAT scores ~137 ΔE vs OKLab ~0.3, observer metamerism) and a physics robustness gate (round-trip + wide-gamut finiteness, measured against the identity so no rival space biases it - it flags e.g. Yrg's 1e-4 round-trip). Color naming (WCS) is omitted - it's space-insensitive (~0.61 everywhere). Colour-science spaces use their canonical reference implementations.
