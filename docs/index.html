ColorBench Leaderboard

Color spaces ranked on real psychophysical datasets — from colour-science and helmlab. Lower score = closer to human vision.

Two boards, on purpose. A space that measures color difference and a space that generates color are judged on different things. Mixing them into one ranking is a category error — so we don't. helmlab isn't one entry either: it competes as metricspace (a learned difference model) on the measurement board and genspace (a synthesis space) on the generation board. Generated by ColorBench · human data from color-perception-datasets ·

Measurement = STRESS on 5 independent difference sources — COMBVD unpacked into its components (BFD-P, Leeds, Witt, RIT-DuPont) plus held-out MacAdam 1974 — each model scoring with its own ΔE; hover a cell for its 95% CI. Overfit Δrank = held-out rank minus mean in-sample rank (+ = relatively better where a model may have been fit). Generation is broken down by gamut (sRGB / Display-P3 / Rec2020): round-trip precision, gamut-mapping hue monotonicity and ΔE smoothness, then gradient evenness and per-dataset human hue / discrimination / spacing; its overall rank weighs each category equally. Rank swing = worst minus best rank across the independent datasets — low means a robust generalist, high means a narrow specialist (the empirical fingerprint of overfitting). A dash means the metric couldn't be measured (e.g. a degenerate gamut-mapping). Colour-science spaces use their canonical reference implementations.
